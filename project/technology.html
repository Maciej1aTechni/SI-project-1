Technology used in the A350 family

Composite materials

Airbus makes these planes from 53% composite materials. They make the plane lighter, corrosion-resistant and metal fatigue-resistant. These reduce the maintenance costs, so the airlines are happy☺️

Rolls-Royce Trent XWB engines

A350 family members are powered by dedicated R&R Trent XWB enignes, which reduce CO2 emmision by 25% compared to older B777 and A340 families. This makes Greenpeace members happy☺️

Smart, adjustable wings

A350 wings are able to adapt their shape, reducing aerodynamic drag. They are controlled by computers. By changing the wings' profile in-flight, airlines can save on fuel, so save money, which make them more happy☺️

Cabin's microclimate

Passengers' comfort is very important for airlines, especially on long-haul travels. A350 has its own microclimate in the cabin. The air is changed every 2-3 minutes - fresh air is taken from outside (even 10-12 km above the ground!), then compressed and heated before being cleand by military HEPA filters, the same as used in operation rooms in hospitals. At the same time, air from inside the plane (the one that contains exhaled CO2) is jettisoned through the back section of the plane. This minimalizes passengers' tireness, which makes them happy☺️

Cockpit

A350's cockpit is one of the most advanced work places in the world. Instead of having milions of switches, pilots have 6 wide LCD screens, which display every needed value: speed, altitude, Angle of Attack and even view from external cameras. Also, pilots have key values displayed directly on the windscreen. This helps them during landing in difficult wather conditions, because they don't have to look down. Also on their screens, pilots can check airport maps and flight plans.

Pilots don't have a classic yoke between their legs as well. Instead, they have a small sidetick mounted next to their knee from outside. When a pilot moves the sidestick, the plane doesn't turn instantly. That is because a pilot isn't moving the flaps/stabilizers alone as in Boeing's planes such as a B737. Instead, the stick input goes to a Fly-By-Wire computer. It calculates how to move control surfaces to turn. It also protects the plane: unless there is a malfunction, the comptuter will not allow a pilot to roll more then 67 degrees sideways or pitch more than 30 degrees up or 15 degrees down.
Those advantages make pilots don't get phisically tired as fast as Boeing pilots do, because they steer with less strengh, which makes them happy☺️
