History of an Airbus A350 family

Cause of origin

At the begining of the 21st century, airlines started to shift their expectations for aircraft. They wanted fuel-efficient ones (to save on fuel costs) instead of giant, quad-engine planes, such an A380. Fisrt to meet those requirements was Boeing with their B777 family. Also in 2004, Boeing started a program to build a new wide-body plane, which ended in creation of a B787 Dreamliner.

Airbus however, was behind Boeing with new wide-body jets, so in 2004 they started working on a new plane that could compete with the B777 and B787 families. The company called it "A350 lite". It was a fuselage of an A330 with new engines. But because of A330's age, CEOs of airlines and leasing companies mocked the idea. So in 2006 Airbus started threw the project away and started working on a completely new plane, the A350 XWB - Xtra Wide-Body. This name is not taken from nowhere - in A350's ecenomy class are 9 seats per row, which made this jet able to compete with Boeing's models.

Timeline

  • July 2006 - announcement of start of the A350 XWB project
  • 13th June 2013 - A350-900 prototype flies for the first time over Airbus' factory in Toulouse, France
  • 15 January 2015 - first commercial flight of the A350-900 (Qatar Airways 67 from Doha to Frankfurt)
  • 24th February 2018 - longer version of an A350-900, an A350-1000, enters serice in Qatar Airways

A350 today

Today, A350 has one of the longest ranges of all planes. In 2026, Qantas (Australian national airlines) chose a modified A350, an A350-1000ULR (Ultra Long Range) to be used on direct flights from Melbourne to London and Paris. Those flights will last over 24 hours and show capabilities of this aircraft. The A350-1000ULR, or, how is it called "Project Sunrise" (Qantas named the project of direct routes mentioned earlier exactly like that, so people started calling the plane itself the same way) has completed a flight from Toulouse to Melbourne and back to Toulouse in July of 2026. Also in 2026, Airbus announced A350F, a cargo version of an A350-1000. This plane made a few test flights over Toulouse and has the biggest cargo doors ever put on an aircraft.
